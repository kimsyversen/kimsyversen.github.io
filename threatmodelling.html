Threat modeling

Think like an attacker while changes are still cheap. Four questions and one drawing take you from idea to a short list of fixes. The example all the way through is a made-up claims portal.

Questions 1 and 2

The claims portal, with its threats

The system
Customers sign in, report damage and upload photos and documents. Claims handlers read them.
In scope
Sign-in, upload and viewing. Not the handlers' internal system.
Worth protecting
Customer documents, customer identities and uptime.
  • External entity
  • Process (running code)
  • Data store
  • Data flow

Draw the system as it is built, not as it was planned. Ask someone who built it to read the drawing.

Question 3

Score and rank

Score = likelihood × impact

Rate each from 1 to 5, then multiply. You get 1 to 25.

  • High, 15 or more. Fix before launch.
  • Middle, 8 to 14. Put in the plan, with a date.
  • Low, 7 or less. Accept or watch, in writing.
Why not DREAD?

DREAD gives five numbers per threat: damage, reproducibility, exploitability, affected users and discoverability. It works, but two numbers mean less debate about what each one means, and they fit a normal risk matrix. Scores are a tool for discussion, not a calculation that is right.

Question 3

Pick one of four responses

Remove

Take away the feature or the data that creates the threat.

EThe portal never opens uploaded files.

Reduce

Add a control that makes it less likely or less harmful.

IAn ownership check on every fetch.

Move

Let someone else carry it, with a ready-made service or insurance.

SSign-in is run by an external provider.

Accept

Only in writing, with a named owner and a date to look again.

RProduct owner, review in six months.

Question 3

Make controls provable (threat I)

Every control gets an owner and a test that shows it works. A control without a test is only a claim.

Question 4

Good enough? Check, then repeat

  • Did we miss something? Walk the drawing again with people who were not in the room. Is there another way to the data, or more people with access?
  • Do the controls work? Run the tests above. Add security testing and code review where you can.
  • What did we learn? Write down what was hard or overlooked, and take it into the next round.

Run it again when

  • You add an integration, a new kind of data or a new trust boundary.
  • Something goes wrong, or you hear of an attack on something similar.
  • An accepted risk reaches its review date.

Done when

  • Scope, assets and drawing are written down.
  • Every threat is one sentence with a score and a level.
  • Every threat has one of the four responses and an owner.
  • Every control has a test, and the test has run.
  • You know when the model comes up again.
