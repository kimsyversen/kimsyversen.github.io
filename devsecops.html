Governance and secure development from planning to retirement.

The delivery lifecycle

This working lifecycle connects governance with secure development. The idea that activities can overlap and repeat, and this phase mapping, are interpretations by AI, not a prescribed or universal sequence. Risks are grouped in the table below.

Governance activities

Delivery phase

Secure development activities

01

Plan

Set the direction

Governance

Classify

Name the risk owner, agree the security level, and record data scope, DPIA need and initial risks.

Secure development

Security requirements

Set scope, acceptance criteria, security and privacy requirements, and likely threats.

02

Design

Shape the solution

Governance

Choose concept

Compare options for architecture and supplier risk. Record the security case, expected residual risk, plan and budget.

Secure development

Threat modeling

Model threats across data flows and integrations. Turn mitigations into owned, testable requirements.

03

Code

Build to the standard

Governance

Ready to build

Confirm the threat model, security backlog, delivery environment, pipeline checks and team ownership are ready.

Secure development

Secure coding and review

Use secure coding standards, review changes, protect secrets and assess dependencies.

04

Build

Package trusted inputs

Governance

Track evidence

Track whether security activities are complete. Keep findings, owners and due dates visible.

Secure development

Build traceability

Package from trusted inputs and keep the artifact traceable to its source and dependencies.

05

Test

Verify the controls

Governance

Review test evidence

Review test evidence against the agreed security requirements and follow unresolved findings to closure.

Secure development

Security testing

Check functionality, quality and security. Scan code, packages, secrets and infrastructure configuration.

06

Release

Approve the version

Governance

Production go / no-go

Check required controls, recovery and monitoring. Resolve critical and high findings before release, or use a time-limited exception approved by the authorized risk owner, with compensating controls where appropriate.

Secure development

Release verification

Approve a verified, traceable software version after required checks pass.

07

Deploy

Deliver with guardrails

Governance

Controlled promotion

Promote only the approved release and confirm the rollout is understood and reversible.

Secure development

Rollout and rollback

Use hardened settings and least-privilege access. Verify the rollout and support rollback.

08

Operate

Run the service

Governance

Handover

Transfer operations and open actions with named owners, dates and the evidence needed to continue.

Secure development

Service maintenance

Maintain reliability, patch vulnerabilities, manage access and backups, and respond to incidents.

09

Monitor

Learn from live signals

Governance

Measure: Improve

Review risk, overdue actions, vulnerability age, tested requirements and recovery results on a set cadence.

Secure development

Detection and alerting

Track service health, performance, user experience and security events. Alert on suspicious activity or change.

10

Retire

Close the lifecycle

Governance

Closeout: Confirm

Assign closure ownership and confirm remaining access and data obligations are addressed.

Secure development

Secure decommissioning

Decommission the service, revoke credentials and access, and handle data under retention requirements.

↰Monitoring, incidents, changes and new vulnerabilities can send work back to planning.

Risks by phase

Select a risk to expand its source evidence.

Possible consequences when governance or secure development activities are missed.
PhaseGovernance risksSecure development risks
01Plan
  • No named risk owner, delaying decisions and leaving business exposure unresolved.
    “Roles, responsibilities, and authorities related to cybersecurity risk management are established, communicated, understood, and enforced.”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RR-02 ↗
  • Critical service goes unclassified, leaving resilience planning and investment out of step with its business importance.
    “Assets are prioritized based on classification, criticality, resources, and impact on the mission.”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · ID.AM-05 ↗
  • Applicable legal and regulatory obligations are not identified or reviewed, increasing the risk of non-compliance.
    “Identify compliance obligations related to the organization's products, services, or activities, and evaluate compliance risks”
    ISO 37301 · ISO/TC 309 FAQ, question 12 ↗
    “Legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements regarding cybersecurity”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.OC-03 ↗

    For example, where GDPR applies:

    “a description of the categories of data subjects and of the categories of personal data”
    GDPR · Article 30 ↗
    “likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons”
    GDPR · Article 35 ↗
    “to ensure and to be able to demonstrate that processing is performed in accordance with this Regulation.”
    GDPR · Article 24 ↗

    These are examples, not a complete list of laws that may apply.
  • Sensitive data left out of scope, leaving it without the intended security controls.
    “protection requirements concerning confidentiality, integrity, availability, and authenticity of all data assets”
    OWASP A06 · Requirements and Resource Management ↗
02Design
  • Risk response left undefined, leaving the business exposed to losses beyond its agreed tolerance.
    “Strategic direction that describes appropriate risk response options is established and communicated.”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RM-04 ↗
  • Abuse paths left open, allowing unauthorized access or actions.
    “Threat modeling is a structured activity for identifying, evaluating, and managing system threats, architectural design flaws, and recommended security mitigations.”
    OWASP SAMM · Threat Modeling ↗
03Code
  • Development starts without security requirements, leading to non-compliance and possibly exploitable vulnerabilities.
    “Identify and document all security requirements for organization-developed software to meet, and maintain the requirements over time.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PO.1.2 ↗
  • Credentials exposed in code, allowing account compromise and unauthorized access.
    “The product contains hard-coded credentials, such as a password or cryptographic key.”
    MITRE CWE-798 · Hard-coded Credentials ↗
  • A compromised or vulnerable dependency enters the product, exposing downstream users to malicious behavior or known flaws.
    “An SSC attack is when a malicious party tampers with the steps, artifacts, or actors within the chain to compromise the consumers of a software artifact down the line.”
    NIST SP 800-204D · Software supply chain attacks ↗
    “Build into the toolchain automatic detection of known vulnerabilities in software components.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PW.4.4 ↗
  • Unnoticed first-party code vulnerabilities, enabling unauthorized access or data theft.
    “Help identify vulnerabilities so that they can be corrected before the software is released to prevent exploitation.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PW.7 ↗
04Build
  • Security findings go untracked, allowing unresolved issues to accumulate and increase delivery delays or incident costs.
    “Record and track all security defects in a defined location.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Tracking ↗
  • Build output tampered with, allowing malicious code to reach deployment.
    “An adversary introduces an unauthorized change to a build output through tampering of the build process”
    SLSA 1.2 · Build Process Threats ↗
05Test
  • Security sign-off lacks test evidence, increasing the chance of a release decision that disrupts the business.
    “consider the passing of security tests as part of merge requirements before allowing new code to enter the main code base.”
    OWASP SAMM · Control Verification ↗
  • Broken controls go unnoticed, leaving exploitable flaws in the release.
    “ensure that the implemented security controls operate as expected and satisfy the project’s stated security requirements”
    OWASP SAMM · Requirements-driven Testing ↗
06Release
  • Accepted risk lacks stakeholder review, exposing the business to losses it did not knowingly accept.
    “all relevant stakeholders have a solid understanding of the imposed risk.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Tracking ↗
  • A substituted artifact ships, deploying unverified or malicious code.
    “SLSA Build L2 covers tampering of the artifact or provenance after the build.”
    SLSA 1.2 · Verifying Artifacts ↗
07Deploy
  • Unapproved changes reach production, risking service disruption, data loss, and recovery costs.
    “Implement approved configuration-controlled changes to the system”
    NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 · CM-3 ↗
  • Insecure settings go live, exposing services through excessive permissions or weakened defenses.
    “missing appropriate security hardening across any part of the application stack or improperly configured permissions on cloud services”
    OWASP A02 · Security Misconfiguration ↗
08Operate
  • Open actions lose ownership at handover, delaying fixes and increasing incident response costs.
    “Roles, responsibilities, and authorities related to cybersecurity risk management are established, communicated, understood, and enforced.”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RR-02 ↗
  • Known flaws stay exploitable, allowing attackers to compromise systems or data.
    “Help ensure that vulnerabilities are remediated in accordance with risk to reduce the window of opportunity for attackers.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · RV.2 ↗
09Monitor
  • Overdue fixes go unreported, allowing manageable issues to grow into costly incidents.
    “centrally monitor and regularly report on SLA breaches.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Tracking ↗
  • Attacks go undetected, allowing intrusions to persist and spread.
    “Without logging and monitoring, attacks and breaches cannot be detected”
    OWASP A09 · Security Logging and Alerting Failures ↗
10Retire
  • Retention obligations remain unresolved, creating unnecessary storage costs and privacy or legal exposure.
    “You have retention requirements for data, and you destroy backups in a timely manner after the relevant retention period ends”
    OWASP SAMM · Data Protection ↗
  • Old credentials still work, allowing access to retired services.
    “When secrets are no longer required or potentially compromised, you must securely revoke them to restrict access.”
    OWASP · Secrets Revocation ↗
  • Data-bearing media is disposed of without suitable sanitization, leaving data recoverable after the service is shut down.
    “Media sanitization refers to a process that renders access to target data on the media infeasible for a given level of effort.”
    NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 · Media Sanitization ↗
