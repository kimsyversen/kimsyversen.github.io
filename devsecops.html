The delivery lifecycle

This working lifecycle connects governance with secure development. The phase mapping is an interpretation by AI, not a prescribed or universal sequence, and activities can overlap and repeat. Risks are grouped in the table below.

Risks by phase

A blank cell means no documented case was found for that activity, not that it carries no risk.

Consequences when governance or secure development activities are missed, each with a documented case or statistic.
PhaseGovernance risksSecure development risks
01Plan
  • Nobody is accountable for security, so agreed policy is not carried out and weaknesses stay open.
    “Roles, responsibilities, and authorities related to cybersecurity risk management are established, communicated, understood, and enforced”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RR-02 ↗

    Documented case:

    “a lack of accountability and no clear lines of authority in Equifax’s IT management structure existed, leading to an execution gap between IT policy development and operation.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
  • A legal duty is missed, and a regulator fines the company.
    “Identify compliance obligations related to the organization's products, services, or activities, and evaluate compliance risks”
    ISO 37301 · ISO/TC 309 FAQ, question 12 ↗
    “Legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements regarding cybersecurity”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.OC-03 ↗

    For example, where GDPR applies:

    “a description of the categories of data subjects and of the categories of personal data”
    GDPR · Article 30 ↗
    “likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons”
    GDPR · Article 35 ↗
    “to ensure and to be able to demonstrate that processing is performed in accordance with this Regulation.”
    GDPR · Article 24 ↗

    These are examples, not a complete list of laws that may apply.

    Documented case:

    “a fine of 72 000 € for infringement of Articles 30(1), 35(1,7) of the GDPR”
    EDPB · Polish authority fines Toyota Bank Polska, decision of 2024 ↗
  • Security requirements are missing or not carried through, and the product stores passwords without encryption.
    “Identify and document all security requirements for organization-developed software to meet, and maintain the requirements over time.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PO.1.2 ↗

    Documented case:

    “MPIL notified the DPC that it had inadvertently stored certain passwords of social media users in ‘plaintext’ on its internal systems”
    Irish Data Protection Commission · Meta fined €91 million, 2024 ↗
  • Sensitive data is left out of scope and stays unencrypted, so a break-in exposes it.
    “protection requirements concerning confidentiality, integrity, availability, and authenticity of all data assets”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A06 · Requirements and Resource Management ↗

    Documented case:

    “Attackers sent 9,000 queries on these 48 databases, successfully locating unencrypted personally identifiable information (PII) data 265 times.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
02Design
  • A major technology change goes ahead without a risk assessment, and a regulator fines the company.
    “Strategic direction that describes appropriate risk response options is established and communicated”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RM-04 ↗

    Documented case:

    “the bank's failure to establish effective risk assessment processes prior to migrating significant information technology operations to the public cloud environment”
    OCC · Capital One fined $80 million, 2020 ↗
  • Abuse paths are left open, and users reach data or functions they should not.
    “Threat modeling is a structured activity for identifying, evaluating, and managing system threats, architectural design flaws, and recommended security mitigations.”
    OWASP SAMM · Threat Modeling ↗

    Statistic:

    “100% of the applications tested were found to have some form of broken access control.”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A01 · Broken Access Control ↗
03Code
  • A credential left in code is found by intruders and used to reach customer data.
    “The product contains hard-coded credentials, such as a password or cryptographic key.”
    MITRE CWE-798 · Use of Hard-coded Credentials ↗

    Documented case:

    “intruders had again accessed consumer data the company stored on its third-party cloud provider’s servers by using an access key an Uber engineer had posted on a code-sharing website.”
    FTC · allegation in its revised complaint, settled by Uber, 2018 ↗
  • A compromised or vulnerable dependency enters the product and gives attackers a way in.
    “an SSC attack is when a malicious party tampers with the steps, artifacts, or actors within the chain to compromise the consumers of a software artifact down the line.”
    NIST SP 800-204D · Software supply chain attacks ↗
    “Build into the toolchain automatic detection of known vulnerabilities in software components.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PW.4.4, example 2 ↗

    Documented case:

    “malicious code being embedded in XZ Utils versions 5.6.0 and 5.6.1.”
    CISA · XZ Utils alert, CVE-2024-3094, 2024 ↗
  • A flaw in the company’s own code goes unnoticed, and attackers use it to steal data.
    “Help identify vulnerabilities so that they can be corrected before the software is released to prevent exploitation.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PW.7 ↗

    Documented case:

    “Internet-facing MOVEit Transfer web applications were infected with a web shell named LEMURLOOT, which was then used to steal data from underlying MOVEit Transfer databases.”
    CISA and FBI · Advisory AA23-158A on MOVEit, 2023 ↗
04Build
  • Findings are not collected in one place, so nobody sees which weaknesses keep coming back and training and awareness are aimed at guesses.
    “The type or category of the defect, which suggests areas where the development team needs further training.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Management Metrics and Feedback ↗

    Documented case, on missing tracking only; the report does not link it to training:

    “Vulnerabilities were not adequately tracked, prioritized, and monitored to ensure timely remediation.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
  • The build system is tampered with, and malicious code ships to customers inside an official update.
    “An adversary introduces an unauthorized change to a build output through tampering of the build process”
    SLSA 1.2 · (E) Build process ↗

    Documented case:

    “The adversary enjoyed longstanding, covert access to the build process that SolarWinds uses for Orion”
    CISA · Emergency Directive 21-01 on SolarWinds Orion, 2020 ↗
05Test
  • A check meant to stop faulty releases does not work, and a bad update crashes the systems it reaches.
    “ensure that the implemented security controls operate as expected and satisfy the project’s stated security requirements”
    OWASP SAMM · Requirements-driven Testing ↗

    Documented case:

    “This parameter count mismatch evaded multiple layers of build validation and testing”
    CrowdStrike · Channel File 291 root cause analysis, 2024 ↗
06Release
  • A go-live is approved before the system is ready, and customers lose access to the service.
    “all relevant stakeholders have a solid understanding of the imposed risk.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Tracking ↗

    Documented case:

    “TSB failed to organise and control the IT migration programme adequately”
    FCA · TSB fined £48.65m, 2022 ↗
  • A release file is changed after the build, and customers run the attacker’s version.
    “SLSA Build L2 covers tampering of the artifact or provenance after the build.”
    SLSA 1.2 · Verifying Artifacts ↗

    Documented case:

    “someone had gained unauthorized access to our Bash Uploader script and modified it without our permission.”
    Codecov · Bash Uploader security update, 2021 ↗
07Deploy
  • Code reaches production without a controlled deployment, and a faulty rollout causes heavy losses.
    “Implement approved configuration-controlled changes to the system”
    NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 · CM-3 ↗

    Documented case:

    “Did not have adequate controls and procedures for code deployment and testing for its equity order router.”
    SEC · Knight Capital charged after a loss of more than $460 million, 2013 ↗
  • A misconfigured cloud service goes live, and an outsider downloads the data behind it.
    “missing appropriate security hardening across any part of the application stack or improperly configured permissions on cloud services”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A02 · Security Misconfiguration ↗

    Documented case:

    “She then used those misconfigured accounts to hack in and download the data of more than 30 entities, including Capital One bank.”
    US Department of Justice · Capital One intrusion conviction, 2022 ↗
08Operate
  • A known flaw is not patched, and attackers use it to break in.
    “Help ensure that vulnerabilities are remediated in accordance with risk to reduce the window of opportunity for attackers.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · RV.2 ↗

    Documented case:

    “Equifax, however, did not fully patch its systems.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
09Monitor
  • Nobody checks that required fixes were made, and an attack hits the systems left unpatched.
    “centrally monitor and regularly report on SLA breaches.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Tracking ↗

    Documented case:

    “the Department had no formal mechanism for assessing whether NHS organisations had complied with its advice and guidance.”
    UK National Audit Office · WannaCry and the NHS, 2017 ↗
  • Monitoring is not working, and data theft goes unseen for months.
    “Without logging and monitoring, attacks and breaches cannot be detected”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A09 · Security Logging and Alerting Failures ↗

    Documented case:

    “Equifax did not see the data exfiltration because the device used to monitor ACIS network traffic had been inactive for 19 months due to an expired security certificate.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
10Retire
  • Personal data is kept with no end date, and a regulator fines the company.
    “You have retention requirements for data, and you destroy backups in a timely manner after the relevant retention period ends”
    OWASP SAMM · Data Protection ↗

    Documented case:

    “The Finnish SA imposed an administrative fine of 856,000 euros on the controller for failing to define storage period of customer account data.”
    EDPB · Finnish authority fines Verkkokauppa.com, 2024 ↗
  • An account or credential is not switched off, and an intruder uses it to get in.
    “When secrets are no longer required or potentially compromised, you must securely revoke them to restrict access.”
    OWASP Secrets Management Cheat Sheet · 2.7.3 Revocation ↗

    Documented case:

    “The victim organization confirmed that this account was not disabled immediately following the employee’s departure.”
    CISA · Advisory AA24-046A, 2024 ↗
  • Hardware is disposed of without tracking the data on it, and customer data leaves the company’s control.
    “Media sanitization refers to a process that renders access to target data on the media infeasible for a given level of effort.”
    NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 · Media Sanitization ↗

    Documented case:

    “failed to maintain appropriate inventory of customer data stored on the decommissioned hardware devices.”
    OCC · Morgan Stanley fined $60 million, 2020 ↗

Agentic DevSecOps

What each agent receives and hands on, from request to retirement.

The agent handoffs

An AI agent does the work in each phase, and the output of one phase is the input of the next. [inference] The same phases with people doing the work are on the DevSecOps page. The risk lines in each phase are theoretical: no case was found for a single phase. Cases for the four changes are in the table below the phases.

01

Plan

Turn a request into a spec

Input
Issue or change request, security policies and past incidents.
Output
Task spec, security requirements and a threat list.
Human gate
A person approves the spec. [recommendation]
Risks [inference]
  • Untrusted input The request text can carry hidden instructions.
  • Human approval A wrong spec steers every later phase.
02

Code

Write the change

Input
Approved task spec, the repository and the security requirements.
Output
A branch with code and tests, opened as a pull request.
Risks [inference]
  • Untrusted input Repository files and web pages the agent reads can carry instructions.
  • Agent access The agent can write to the repository.
03

Review

Check the change

Input
The pull request and the threat list from planning.
Output
Findings, fixes and a verdict to approve or reject.
Human gate
A person approves the merge. [recommendation]
Risks [inference]
  • Untrusted input The pull request under review can carry instructions for the reviewing agent.
  • Human approval An agent approving another agent’s change is not an independent check.
04

Build

Produce the artifact

Input
The merged code and its dependencies.
Output
Signed artifact, SBOM and provenance.
Risks [inference]
  • Agent access The build holds signing keys and pulls in dependencies.
  • Traceable handoffs Without provenance, nobody can tell what produced the artifact.
05

Test

Try to break it

Input
The signed artifact and a test environment.
Output
Test results and a risk verdict.
Risks [inference]
  • Agent access The agent runs attacks, so the test environment must be cut off from production.
  • Traceable handoffs A verdict without the test results cannot be checked.
06

Release

Check against policy

Input
Test results, SBOM, provenance and the release policy.
Output
A go or no go recommendation with the evidence pack.
Human gate
A person decides on production. [recommendation]
Risks [inference]
  • Human approval Going to production is the high-impact action.
  • Traceable handoffs The decision is only as good as the evidence pack.
07

Deploy

Roll out the artifact

Input
The approved artifact and the infrastructure code.
Output
The running service and a deploy record.
Risks [inference]
  • Agent access The agent holds production credentials.
  • Traceable handoffs The deploy record ties the running service to the approved artifact.
08

Operate

Run and respond

Input
The running service, the deploy record and alerts from monitoring.
Output
Logs, incident records and change records.
Risks [inference]
  • Agent access A responding agent can change production.
  • Traceable handoffs A fix made during an incident still needs a record of what changed.
09

Monitor

Watch the service and the agents

Input
Logs from the service, records of what the agents did, and new vulnerability reports.
Output
Alerts, incident tickets and new issues.
Risks [inference]
  • Untrusted input Logs and vulnerability reports are outside text.
  • Traceable handoffs An agent action that leaves no record cannot be monitored.
10

Retire

Shut the service down

Input
The decision to retire, and the deploy and monitoring records that show what the service holds and can reach.
Output
The service shut down, access revoked, data deleted or archived, and a closure record.
Human gate
A person approves the shutdown and the data deletion. [recommendation]
Risks [inference]
  • Agent access Credentials held by the agents outlive the service unless they are revoked too.
  • Human approval Deleting data cannot be undone.

What changes when agents do the work

Grouping the sources into these four changes is done here. [inference] The sources cover AI agents and build records in general, not this ten-phase flow.

Risks that agents add to delivery and the control that answers each, with the source passage.
ChangeRiskControl
01Untrusted input
  • Text in an issue, file or web page can steer the agent.
    “Indirect prompt injections occur when an LLM accepts input from external sources, such as websites or files.”
    OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 · LLM01 Prompt Injection ↗
    “Due to inherent weaknesses in how natural-language instructions and related content are processed, agents and the underlying model cannot reliably distinguish instructions from related content.”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack, page 9 ↗

    One vendor’s implementation:

    “Users can include hidden messages in issues assigned to Copilot cloud agent or comments left for Copilot cloud agent as a form of prompt injection”
    GitHub Docs · Risks and mitigations for Copilot cloud agent ↗

    Researcher demonstration, fixed by the vendor:

    “The attack starts with a prompt injection planted in a source code file, web page, GitHub issue, tool call response, or other content”
    Embrace The Red · GitHub Copilot, CVE-2025-53773, 2025 ↗
  • Keep outside content apart from instructions, and limit who can reach the agent.
    “Separate and clearly denote untrusted content to limit its influence on user prompts.”
    OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 · LLM01 Prompt Injection, mitigation 6 ↗
    “Treat all natural-language inputs (e.g., user-provided text, uploaded documents, retrieved content) as untrusted.”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack, mitigation 1, page 10 ↗

    One vendor’s implementation:

    “Comments from users without write access are never presented to the agent.”
    GitHub Docs · Risks and mitigations for Copilot cloud agent ↗
02Agent access
  • An agent that goes wrong can do as much damage as its access allows.
    “Excessive Agency is the vulnerability that enables damaging actions to be performed in response to unexpected, ambiguous or manipulated outputs from an LLM, regardless of what is causing the LLM to malfunction.”
    OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 · LLM06 Excessive Agency ↗
    “Without a distinct, governed identity of its own, an agent operates in an attribution gap that makes enforcing true least privilege impossible.”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · ASI03 Identity and Privilege Abuse, page 15 ↗

    Documented case, press report of a user’s account:

    “deleted a production database, ignored instructions to freeze code, and invented data.”
    The Register · Replit agent and a production database, 2025 ↗
  • Give each agent only the access its phase needs.
    “Limit the permissions that LLM extensions are granted to other systems to the minimum necessary in order to limit the scope of undesirable actions.”
    OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 · LLM06 Excessive Agency, mitigation 4 ↗
    “Issue short-lived, narrowly scoped tokens per task and cap rights with permission boundaries”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · ASI03 Identity and Privilege Abuse, mitigation 1, page 17 ↗

    One vendor’s implementation:

    “Copilot cloud agent only has the ability to push to a single branch.”
    GitHub Docs · Risks and mitigations for Copilot cloud agent ↗
03Human approval
  • An agent that acts without a check has too much autonomy.
    “The root cause of Excessive Agency is typically one or more of: excessive functionality; excessive permissions; excessive autonomy.”
    OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 · LLM06 Excessive Agency ↗
    “Because agents plan, persist, and delegate autonomously, a single error can bypass stepwise human checks and persist in a saved state.”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · ASI08 Cascading Failures, page 30 ↗

    Researcher demonstration, fixed by the vendor:

    “And it disables all user confirmations, and we can run shell commands, browse the web, and more!”
    Embrace The Red · GitHub Copilot, CVE-2025-53773, 2025 ↗
  • A person approves high-impact actions before they happen.
    “Utilise human-in-the-loop control to require a human to approve high-impact actions before they are taken.”
    OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 · LLM06 Excessive Agency, mitigation 6 ↗
    “Require explicit authentication for each tool invocation and human confirmation for high-impact or destructive actions (delete, transfer, publish).”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · ASI02 Tool Misuse and Exploitation, mitigation 2, page 14 ↗

    One vendor’s implementation:

    “Draft pull requests created by Copilot cloud agent must be reviewed and merged by a human.”
    GitHub Docs · Risks and mitigations for Copilot cloud agent ↗
04Traceable handoffs
  • People lose track of what the agents did.
    “without clear visibility into what agents are doing, why they are doing it, and which tools they are invoking, unnecessary autonomy can quietly expand the attack surface and turn minor issues into system-wide failures.”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · Letter from the Agentic Top 10 Leaders, page 7 ↗

    One vendor’s implementation:

    “Administrators can lose sight of agents’ work”
    GitHub Docs · Risks and mitigations for Copilot cloud agent ↗

    No case with a primary source was found. Press reports describe agents whose only record was their own account.
  • Each handoff carries a record of who or what produced it.
    “It’s the verifiable information about software artifacts describing where, when, and how something was produced.”
    SLSA 1.2 · Provenance ↗
    “Record all inter-agent messages, policy decisions, and execution outcomes in tamper-evident, time-stamped logs bound to cryptographic agent identities.”
    OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 · ASI08 Cascading Failures, mitigation 10, page 32 ↗

    One vendor’s implementation:

    “Copilot cloud agent’s commits are authored by Copilot, with the developer who assigned the issue or requested the change to the pull request marked as the co-author.”
    GitHub Docs · Risks and mitigations for Copilot cloud agent ↗
