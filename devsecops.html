The delivery lifecycle

01Plan
  • Product and IT have no clear owner for enforcing security policy, so known weaknesses stay open.

    Direct for accountability. The House investigation found unclear lines of authority and a gap between Equifax's IT policies and how teams carried them out. House Oversight report, PDF p. 4

    “Roles, responsibilities, and authorities related to cybersecurity risk management are established, communicated, understood, and enforced”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RR-02 ↗

    Documented case:

    “a lack of accountability and no clear lines of authority in Equifax’s IT management structure existed, leading to an execution gap between IT policy development and operation.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
  • A project misses a privacy duty when defining how it will use personal data, so it leaves required records or assessments incomplete and can be fined.

    Direct. The Polish authority fined Toyota Bank Polska for leaving profiling out of its records of processing and data protection impact assessment. Polish Data Protection Office

    “Identify compliance obligations related to the organization's products, services, or activities, and evaluate compliance risks”
    ISO 37301 · ISO/TC 309 FAQ, question 12 ↗
    “Legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements regarding cybersecurity”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.OC-03 ↗

    For example, where GDPR applies:

    “a description of the categories of data subjects and of the categories of personal data”
    GDPR · Article 30 ↗
    “likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons”
    GDPR · Article 35 ↗
    “to ensure and to be able to demonstrate that processing is performed in accordance with this Regulation.”
    GDPR · Article 24 ↗

    These are examples, not a complete list of laws that may apply.

    Documented case:

    “a fine of 72 000 € for infringement of Articles 30(1), 35(1,7) of the GDPR”
    EDPB · Polish authority fines Toyota Bank Polska, decision of 2024 ↗
  • The business depends on one provider and has no tested fallback, so losing that provider can stop essential work.

    Partial. UnitedHealth reported widespread payment and claims disruption after the Change Healthcare shutdown. The testimony leaves individual customers' contingency plans unknown. Testimony to Congress, 2024
  • The requirements miss password protection in storage and logs, so the software can record passwords in plain text.

    Partial. Ireland's DPC found that Meta stored some user passwords in plain text. The DOJ separately documented that a password retrieved in plain text from a federal portal database was used to access a complainant's email. One case establishes plaintext storage and the other establishes retrieval and misuse. The original requirements remain unknown. NIST specifies storage protections for centrally verified passwords. Irish DPC decision summary, 2024 · DOJ conviction announcement, 2026 · NIST SP 800-63B-4, password verifiers

    “Identify and document all security requirements for organization-developed software to meet, and maintain the requirements over time.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PO.1.2 ↗

    Documented case, on plaintext storage only; the DPC cited Articles 5(1)(f), 32(1), 33(1) and 33(5), not missing requirements:

    “MPIL notified the DPC that it had inadvertently stored certain passwords of social media users in ‘plaintext’ on its internal systems”
    Irish Data Protection Commission · Meta fined €91 million, 2024 ↗
  • The project misses a location that holds personal data, so an intruder who reaches it can find readable records.

    Partial. The House report records that sensitive data at Equifax was unencrypted. It leaves the original requirements and the protection that would have stopped the theft unknown. House Oversight report, PDF pp. 3-4

    “protection requirements concerning confidentiality, integrity, availability, and authenticity of all data assets”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A06 · Requirements and Resource Management ↗

    Documented case, on unencrypted data only; the report does not say it was left out of scope:

    “Attackers sent 9,000 queries on these 48 databases, successfully locating unencrypted personally identifiable information (PII) data 265 times.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
02Design
  • A major migration goes ahead without assessing how it changes security risks, so known weaknesses stay open and the company can face enforcement.

    Direct. The OCC cited ineffective pre-migration risk assessment processes and untimely correction of deficiencies in its $80 million Capital One penalty. The OCC release leaves the options considered unknown. OCC, 2020

    “Strategic direction that describes appropriate risk response options is established and communicated”
    NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RM-04 ↗

    Documented case:

    “the bank's failure to establish effective risk assessment processes prior to migrating significant information technology operations to the public cloud environment”
    OCC · Capital One fined $80 million, 2020 ↗
  • A supplier integration can read sensitive customer records, so a compromise of its access credentials can expose data from connected companies.

    Direct for the access and theft. Google's incident responders documented data theft from Salesforce tenants through stolen OAuth tokens associated with Salesloft Drift. The tenants' supplier reviews and available permission choices remain unknown. Google Cloud Threat Horizons, H1 2026
  • The design leaves a gap in access controls, so a user or service reaches restricted data or functions.

    Direct for the boundary failure. Microsoft documented that its enterprise mail system accepted tokens signed with a consumer key. Investigators did not establish how the attacker obtained the key. Microsoft investigation, updated 2024

    “Threat modeling is a structured activity for identifying, evaluating, and managing system threats, architectural design flaws, and recommended security mitigations.”
    OWASP SAMM · Threat Modeling ↗

    Statistic, not tied to threat modeling; the source does not give a cause:

    “100% of the applications tested were found to have some form of broken access control.”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A01 · Broken Access Control ↗
  • Several services rely on the same component without a working fallback, so one failure can disrupt them at the same time.

    Direct. A failure of the storage behind Cloudflare Workers KV disrupted multiple products. Cloudflare identified reliance on a single storage provider as a design weakness. Cloudflare incident report, 12 June 2025

  • A website loads code a supplier can change, so a compromise at the supplier can put malicious code in visitors' browsers without a new release from the website team.

    Direct. Optimizely reported that injected project JavaScript ran in some affected websites' visitors' browsers in November 2026. The investigation scopes the affected projects and documents exposure. Optimizely incident report, 2026
03Code
  • The company lets a staff account with weak sign-in protection access a repository, so someone who takes over the account can find credentials for production data.

    Complaint. The FTC alleged that Drizly did not require multifactor authentication for personal GitHub accounts with access to its repositories. It alleged that an intruder used a compromised account to obtain credentials and access production data. The FTC subsequently finalized a consent order. FTC complaint, paragraphs 18-26 · FTC case and final order
  • A working cloud credential is left in code or its history, and an intruder who finds it uses it to reach customer data.

    Complaint. The FTC alleged this path in the revised Uber complaint that led to a settlement. FTC settlement announcement, 2018

    “The product contains hard-coded credentials, such as a password or cryptographic key.”
    MITRE CWE-798 · Use of Hard-coded Credentials ↗

    Documented case:

    “intruders had again accessed consumer data the company stored on its third-party cloud provider’s servers by using an access key an Uber engineer had posted on a code-sharing website.”
    FTC · allegation in its revised complaint, settled by Uber, 2018 ↗
  • A product takes a compromised or vulnerable dependency, so customers inherit malicious code or a security flaw they did not introduce.

    Compromise and vulnerable component case. Red Hat traced a backdoor to affected releases of XZ Utils. Australian cyber authorities documented exploitation of the Log4j library in real incidents. The XZ source describes the compromised releases. The Log4j source illustrates exposure through a software dependency. Red Hat XZ review, 2024 · Australian Cyber Security Centre annual threat report, 2022

    “an SSC attack is when a malicious party tampers with the steps, artifacts, or actors within the chain to compromise the consumers of a software artifact down the line.”
    NIST SP 800-204D · Software supply chain attacks ↗
    “Build into the toolchain automatic detection of known vulnerabilities in software components.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PW.4.4, example 2 ↗

    Documented case:

    “malicious code being embedded in XZ Utils versions 5.6.0 and 5.6.1.”
    CISA · XZ Utils alert, CVE-2024-3094, 2024 ↗
  • A database input flaw goes unnoticed in code the company ships or relies on, and attackers use it to steal stored data.

    Direct for the flaw and data access. Progress identified a SQL injection flaw in MOVEit Transfer and reported that attackers exploited it to gain unauthorized access to data. The vendor's review and testing history remains unknown. Progress patch FAQ · Progress SEC annual filing, 2024

    “Help identify vulnerabilities so that they can be corrected before the software is released to prevent exploitation.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · PW.7 ↗

    Documented case, a zero-day in a vendor product:

    “Internet-facing MOVEit Transfer web applications were infected with a web shell named LEMURLOOT, which was then used to steal data from underlying MOVEit Transfer databases.”
    CISA and FBI · Advisory AA23-158A on MOVEit, 2023 ↗
  • A bug in how the application handles data across requests sends one customer's information to another during normal use.

    Direct. OpenAI traced the 20 March 2023 ChatGPT exposure to a Redis client bug and reported exposure of some users' chat titles or payment-related information. The defect became visible under specific runtime conditions. OpenAI incident report, 2023

  • A compromised editor extension runs on a developer's computer, and an intruder uses that access to steal source code.

    Direct. GitHub attributed an employee-device compromise and theft of internal repositories to a third-party VS Code extension. GitHub rotated a signing key as a precaution and verified its hosted binaries. GitHub investigation, 2026
04Build
  • Teams record build findings without owners or due dates, so they lose track of what needs fixing and which problems keep recurring.

    Partial. The House investigation found inadequate vulnerability tracking, prioritization and follow-up at Equifax. It concerns vulnerability management across operations. NIST SSDF recommends recording and triaging vulnerabilities, including those found by automated tools. House Oversight report, PDF p. 69 · NIST SSDF 1.1, RV.1 and RV.2

    “The type or category of the defect, which suggests areas where the development team needs further training.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Management Metrics and Feedback ↗

    Documented case, on missing tracking only; the report does not link it to training:

    “Vulnerabilities were not adequately tracked, prioritized, and monitored to ensure timely remediation.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
  • Nobody tracks which dependencies are in each release or where it runs, so teams scramble to find affected systems when a dependency flaw is reported.

    Partial. Australian cyber authorities documented incidents involving Log4j and described the difficulty caused by unknown ICT assets and dependencies. Its findings support the discovery problem. A missing build inventory at a specific victim remains unverified. Australian Cyber Security Centre annual threat report, 2022
  • Attackers tamper with the build system, and malicious code ships to customers inside a release they trust.

    Direct. SolarWinds reported compromise of its Orion build process and distribution of affected software updates. SolarWinds SEC filing, 2021

    “An adversary introduces an unauthorized change to a build output through tampering of the build process”
    SLSA 1.2 · (E) Build process ↗

    Documented case:

    “The adversary enjoyed longstanding, covert access to the build process that SolarWinds uses for Orion”
    CISA · ED 21-01 Supplemental Guidance v3 on SolarWinds Orion, 2021 ↗
  • The build system holds credentials for several projects, so a break-in there gives an intruder access to other repositories, cloud services, or release systems.

    Direct. CircleCI reported unauthorized access to customer environment variables, tokens and keys. AWS separately attributed an unauthorized Amazon Q extension change to a build integration token with excessive scope. CircleCI postmortem, 2023 · AWS security bulletin, 2025

  • A CI job that runs unreviewed code shares state or permissions with the release build, so it can publish a compromised package under the project's name.

    Direct. TanStack reported that a workflow trust-boundary failure let an attacker misuse a short-lived publishing identity. It documented 84 malicious versions across 42 packages. TanStack postmortem, 2026
05Test
  • A team skips a required test without raising the decision, so the release approver cannot see what remains untested.

    Direct for testing governance. The FCA found that TSB omitted active-active performance testing, failed to assess the risks of that omission, and bypassed the expected testing forum. FCA final notice, sections 2.34-2.35 and 5.4-5.5
  • A check misses that an update is incompatible with the software that reads it, and the update crashes the systems it reaches.

    Direct. CrowdStrike's Channel File 291 analysis identifies the validation and testing gaps that preceded a Windows outage. The case concerns reliability of security software's content update. CrowdStrike root cause analysis, 2024

    “ensure that the implemented security controls operate as expected and satisfy the project’s stated security requirements”
    OWASP SAMM · Requirements-driven Testing ↗

    Documented case, a testing gap in a content update, not a security-control test:

    “This parameter count mismatch evaded multiple layers of build validation and testing”
    CrowdStrike · Channel File 291 root cause analysis, 2024 ↗
  • A team copies real customer data into a test system with weaker protections, and outsiders can access the copy.

    Complaint. The FTC alleged that identifiable customer data in a Global Tel*Link test environment was accessible over the internet without password protection. The FTC later finalized its consent order. FTC complaint, paragraphs 21-33 · FTC final order announcement, 2024

  • Tests miss how a security feature behaves under realistic load, and the feature slows or takes down the live service when rolled out.

    Direct. A Cloudflare WAF rule passed the standard tests, which lacked a check for excessive CPU use. The resulting outage was amplified by global rollout. Cloudflare incident report, 2 July 2019

  • Tests check that allowed requests work and skip requests from the wrong account, so an access flaw reaches production and lets users change another customer's resources.

    Direct. Phala Cloud reported that an ownership check was missing from AI-assisted code. Normal tests and review missed it, and an attacker changed resources they did not own. Phala Cloud postmortem, 2026
06Release
  • The team approves a release with gaps in its risk and recovery checks, and customers lose access while the team struggles to restore service.

    Direct for readiness and impact. TSB considered attestations, test evidence and residual risks. The FCA found gaps in risk management and preparation for the scale of post-migration defects. The decision and approvals are described in the full notice. FCA final notice, sections J and 4.265 onward

    “all relevant stakeholders have a solid understanding of the imposed risk.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Tracking ↗

    Documented case, on poor planning and control of the migration; the FCA does not describe the go-live decision:

    “TSB failed to organise and control the IT migration programme adequately”
    FCA and PRA · TSB fined £48.65m together, 2022 ↗
  • A release goes ahead under an undocumented exception, so teams cannot verify who accepted the risk or when they must meet the conditions.

    Supervisory finding. The FCA observed an AI use case going to production under an undocumented exception and observed failures to track conditional approvals across its wholesale-bank review. The report documents the governance failure. Customer harm remains unknown. FCA wholesale-bank review, autumn 2026
  • A release file is changed after the build, and customers run the attacker's version under the original name.

    Direct. Codecov reported that its published Bash Uploader was modified and that a customer spotted a checksum mismatch. HashiCorp disclosed exposure of a signing key and rotated it. Unauthorized use of that key was unverified. Codecov incident notice, 2021 · HashiCorp customer impact advisory, 2021

    “SLSA Build L2 covers tampering of the artifact or provenance after the build.”
    SLSA 1.2 · Verifying Artifacts ↗

    Documented case:

    “someone had gained unauthorized access to our Bash Uploader script and modified it without our permission.”
    Codecov · Bash Uploader security update, 2021 ↗
  • A registry accepts a new version published outside the approved build process, and customers can install it under the project's trusted name.

    Direct for publication. The Axios maintainer confirmed that a compromised account published two new malicious npm versions. The notice identifies the affected versions and the publication window. Axios maintainer postmortem, 2026
07Deploy
  • Nobody verifies that every production system got the approved version, so a partial rollout goes undetected and causes losses.

    Direct. The SEC found inadequate controls for deploying and testing Knight Capital's order router. One server was missed in deployment and the firm lost more than $460 million. SEC Knight Capital order, 2013

    “Implement approved configuration-controlled changes to the system”
    NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 · CM-3 ↗

    Documented case:

    “Did not have adequate controls and procedures for code deployment and testing for its equity order router.”
    SEC · Knight Capital charged after a loss of more than $460 million, 2013 ↗
  • A misconfigured production access control gives an intruder a way to reach customer data.

    Direct. The DOJ linked unauthorized access to data in the Capital One intrusion to a misconfigured web application firewall. The date the setting was introduced remains unknown. DOJ conviction announcement, 2022

    “missing appropriate security hardening across any part of the application stack or improperly configured permissions on cloud services”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A02 · Security Misconfiguration ↗

    Documented case:

    “She then used those misconfigured accounts to hack in and download the data of more than 30 entities, including Capital One bank.”
    US Department of Justice · Capital One intrusion conviction, 2022 ↗
  • A faulty configuration reaches several regions before teams can stop the rollout, and failure in a shared service can slow recovery.

    Direct. Google Cloud reported that a globally replicated policy update interacted with a recently released Service Control change. Multiple services were disrupted, and clearing the problematic state took longer in a large region. Google Cloud incident report, 12 June 2025

  • A cloud resource is set to expire by mistake, so it is deleted across the zones meant to keep it running.

    Direct. Google traced deletion of one UniSuper private cloud, spanning two zones, to a term incorrectly set at provisioning. Separately stored backups and third-party backup software helped recovery. Google Cloud GCVE incident report, 2024

  • A team deletes the old database before checking data integrity after a migration, so missing records take longer to recover.

    Direct. LootLocker reported missing writes after a cutover and that it deleted the source database before discovering the gap. It later restored the source and replayed the identified records. LootLocker migration postmortem, 2026
08Operate
  • Operations stops rotating signing keys without establishing a replacement, so old keys stay trusted.

    Direct for the maintenance gap. The Cyber Safety Review Board found that Microsoft paused manual consumer signing-key rotation after an outage and lacked automated rotation or key-age alerting before the intrusion. The initial key acquisition remained unknown. Cyber Safety Review Board report in the congressional record, PDF p. 16

  • The support plan underestimates how many failures can happen at once, so the team takes longer to restore service.

    Direct for support preparedness. TSB recognized a post-go-live defect volume risk. The FCA found the bank was inadequately prepared for the scale at which it materialized. FCA final notice, section 4.265 onward

  • A critical system stays in service after its vendor stops providing security fixes, so attackers can exploit a flaw for which the team has no supported patch.

    Direct for unsupported equipment. The DOJ reported that most routers in the KV botnet were past vendor support. The case concerns small-office and home-office devices. UK financial authorities separately highlighted end-of-life systems and loss of vendor support as resourcing issues. DOJ botnet disruption, 2024 · FCA, Bank of England and Treasury statement, 2026
  • A known flaw is left unpatched on a live system, and attackers use it to break in.

    Direct. The Equifax investigation linked the breach to an unpatched Apache Struts vulnerability. House Oversight report, PDF pp. 2-3

    “Help ensure that vulnerabilities are remediated in accordance with risk to reduce the window of opportunity for attackers.”
    NIST SSDF 1.1 · RV.2 ↗

    Documented case:

    “Equifax, however, did not fully patch its systems.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
  • A team leaves some service credentials active after a supplier breach, and an intruder reuses them to get in.

    Direct. Cloudflare traced access to its systems to credentials it had missed when rotating secrets after the Okta incident. Cloudflare Thanksgiving 2023 incident report

  • Backups stop without an alert or fail when needed at scale, so the team cannot recover all the data after a production failure.

    Direct. GitLab could not use several expected recovery paths after a database incident and recovered from an older copy, with data loss. GitLab database outage postmortem, 2017
09Monitor
  • Nobody checks that required fixes reached every affected system, so vulnerable services stay exposed after the fixes were supposed to be complete.

    Direct. The UK National Audit Office found no formal mechanism to assess local NHS compliance with national advice before WannaCry. The report records disruption across the NHS. NAO report, PDF pp. 7-9

    “centrally monitor and regularly report on SLA breaches.”
    OWASP SAMM · Defect Tracking ↗

    Documented case:

    “the Department had no formal mechanism for assessing whether NHS organisations had complied with its advice and guidance.”
    UK National Audit Office · WannaCry and the NHS, 2017 ↗
  • The company delays telling affected customers about a breach, so they lose time to protect their accounts and data.

    Complaint and final order. The FTC alleged that CafePress concealed a breach from affected customers. It finalized an order requiring consumer notification and security measures. FTC final action, 2022

  • An outsourced security team has unclear rules for escalating alerts, so the company responds after attackers have had time to steal data.

    Direct. The SEC found inadequate alert review and escalation arrangements at R.R. Donnelley. Its systems began alerting about three weeks before the eventual encryption and theft of data. SEC order, paragraphs 5-17

  • A manufacturer fails to recognize that an exploited product flaw is reportable, so it misses the deadline for warning authorities.

    Obligation. For manufacturers in scope, EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting began on 11 September 2026. Covered actively exploited product vulnerabilities and severe security incidents have a 24-hour early-warning deadline from awareness. This source establishes the deadline. European Commission CRA reporting guidance

  • A new security report is closed as a duplicate, so the additional exposure stays open until the issue is disclosed publicly.

    Direct. ClickUp reported that a materially different submission revealing customer email addresses and a live API token was incorrectly closed. ClickUp learned of the details on public disclosure. It reported no evidence that anyone beyond the researcher used the token. ClickUp incident update, 2026
  • Security monitoring fails without an alert, and data theft goes unseen while the service appears to be working.

    Direct. Equifax's traffic inspection had been blind for 19 months because of an expired certificate. The investigation distinguishes that period from the shorter period of the breach. House Oversight report, PDF p. 3

    “Without logging and monitoring, attacks and breaches cannot be detected”
    OWASP Top 10:2025 A09 · Security Logging and Alerting Failures ↗

    Documented case:

    “Equifax did not see the data exfiltration because the device used to monitor ACIS network traffic had been inactive for 19 months due to an expired security certificate.”
    US House Oversight Committee · Equifax breach report, 2018 ↗
  • Security logs are missing or unavailable to the people investigating, so they cannot tell what happened or who was affected.

    Direct. The CSRB found that the Commerce Department could not use newly enabled Microsoft logging to investigate earlier activity. Cyber Safety Review Board report in the congressional record, PDF pp. 20-21
10Retire
  • Nobody sets when to delete personal data, so it is kept indefinitely and the company can be fined.

    Direct for retention. Finland’s privacy authority fined Verkkokauppa.com for failing to define a customer-account data retention period. In 2026, the Supreme Administrative Court upheld the retention finding and left the reduced €792,639 penalty in place. The case concerns ongoing retention independently of a service shutdown. EDPB case summary, 2024 · Supreme Administrative Court decision, 2026

    “You have retention requirements for data, and you destroy backups in a timely manner after the relevant retention period ends”
    OWASP SAMM · Data Protection ↗

    Documented case:

    “The Finnish SA imposed an administrative fine of 856,000 euros on the controller for failing to define storage period of customer account data.”
    EDPB · Finnish authority fines Verkkokauppa.com, 2024 ↗
  • A company hires a disposal vendor without checking its ability to destroy data securely, and is held responsible when customer records leave its control.

    Direct. The SEC found that Morgan Stanley Smith Barney hired a moving and storage company without data-destruction experience and failed to oversee disposal. Equipment with customer data later reached resale. SEC order announcement, 2022

  • A cleanup relies on retention settings the company has never verified, so it deletes records the company must keep.

    Direct for records cleanup. The SEC found that about 47 million J.P. Morgan Securities communications were permanently deleted after the archiving vendor failed to apply the expected retention settings in one domain. Some communications fell within the regulatory retention period. The case concerns records cleanup during operation. SEC order, paragraphs 5-11
  • An account or credential is left active after an employee leaves or a service shuts down, and an intruder uses it to get in.

    Direct for employee offboarding. The DOJ reported that a former medical center employee used an administrator account after termination, deleted files and disrupted operations. A service credential left active after shutdown has the same revocation gap. DOJ sentencing announcement, 2021

    “When secrets are no longer required or potentially compromised, you must securely revoke them to restrict access.”
    OWASP Secrets Management Cheat Sheet · 2.7.3 Revocation ↗

    Documented case:

    “The victim organization confirmed that this account was not disabled immediately following the employee’s departure.”
    CISA · Advisory AA24-046A, 2024 ↗
  • The disposal team loses track of which devices still hold customer data, and equipment with readable data is sold or discarded.

    Direct. The OCC cited Morgan Stanley's inventory failure. The SEC's later order found devices containing customer data were sold and ultimately resold, with some reaching an online auction. OCC penalty, 2020 · SEC order and announcement, 2022

    “Media sanitization refers to a process that renders access to target data on the media infeasible for a given level of effort.”
    NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 · Media Sanitization ↗

    Documented case, on the missing inventory only; the quote does not say the data left the bank’s control:

    “failed to maintain appropriate inventory of customer data stored on the decommissioned hardware devices.”
    OCC · Morgan Stanley fined $60 million, 2020 ↗
  • A shutdown script is given the wrong list of systems, so it deletes live customer services that the team has to restore.

    Direct. Atlassian intended to remove a legacy standalone app, but a target-list mix-up resulted in deletion of 883 cloud sites. Atlassian post-incident review, 2022

  • Old DNS or email records still point to an abandoned service, and someone takes it over and sends phishing mail under the company's name.

    Direct. Guardio Labs documented a malicious campaign that used abandoned dependencies in DNS and email records to send spam and phishing mail under trusted names. Guardio Labs SubdoMailing investigation, 2024

The merged code and its dependencies.
Output

Try to break it

Input
The signed artifact, the tests from the pull request (carried over from Code) and a test environment.
Output
Test results and a risk verdict.

Check against policy

Input
Test results and risk verdict, the signed artifact, SBOM, provenance and the release policy.
Output
A go or no go recommendation with the evidence pack.

Deploy

Roll out the artifact

Input
The approved artifact and the infrastructure code.
Output
The running service and a deploy record.
08

Operate

Run and respond

Input
The running service, the deploy record, the runbook and alerts from monitoring.
Output
Logs, incident records and change records.
09

Monitor

Watch the service and the agents

Input
Logs from the service, records of what the agents did, and new vulnerability reports.
Output
Alerts, incident tickets and new issues.

Shut the service down

Input
The decision to retire, and the infrastructure code, change records and the list of credentials the agents hold, which show what the service holds and can reach.

Have a person approve the shutdown and what happens to the data.

Example: fixing one bug with agents

A made-up fix on the claims portal from the threat modelling page. Issue 214: a customer can open other customers’ documents by changing the ID in the address. [inference]

PhaseWhat the agent doesWhat it hands on
01Plan Reads issue 214 and the threat register. Writes a spec: on every document fetch the server checks that the logged-in customer owns the document, and answers no if the check fails or breaks. The spec with two tests, and the threat list. Customer A asks for customer B’s document and is refused. The check is made to throw an error and nothing is delivered.

Human gateThe product owner reads the spec and approves it.
02Code Works on its own branch and cannot reach production. Adds the ownership check to the fetch handler and writes the two tests. Pull request 231, linked to issue 214, with both tests passing.
03Review A second agent reads the pull request against the threat list. It finds that downloading several documents at once skips the check. The coding agent fixes that and adds a third test. The finding, the fix and a verdict: approve.

Human gateA developer reads the diff and the finding, then merges.
04Build Builds the merged commit in a separate build service, which signs the result. Signed artifact, SBOM and provenance that names the commit and the builder.
05Test Starts the artifact in a test environment with made-up customers. Runs the three tests, then asks for 200 documents that belong to other customers, one by one and in bulk downloads. Test results: the three tests passed and all 200 requests were refused. Risk verdict: low for this bug, no other behaviour tested.
06Release Checks the evidence against the release policy: the tests passed, the signature is valid and the provenance points to the merged commit. A recommendation to go, with the evidence pack.

Human gateThe release owner reads the pack and approves production.
07Deploy Rolls out the approved artifact with a credential that works for this deploy only. The running service and a deploy record: artifact digest and time.
08Operate Runs the service. When Monitor alerts on one account, it ends that account’s sessions, a low-impact step the runbook allows without approval. An incident record and a change record of what it did.
09Monitor Reads the service logs and the agents’ records. One account is refused 50 times in ten minutes, so it alerts Operate. The alert, and issue 240: limit how fast one account can fetch documents. The issue goes back to Plan.
10Retire Not part of this change. When the portal is replaced, an agent lists what the portal holds and can reach, from the infrastructure code, the change records and the agents’ credential list. A shutdown plan. After approval: the portal shut down, the agents’ credentials revoked, the documents archived and a closure record.

Human gateHave a person approve the shutdown and what happens to the documents.

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AC-G1 · Governance A development agent is given access to the live database, and it deletes production data while working on a development task. Operational case. SaaStr account, 2025 · Replit account
AC-G2 · Governance A developer sends source code to an AI tool the company has not approved for that data, so the company can lose control of where the code is stored or used. Field data. Verizon's 2026 DBIR ranked source code as the most common data type in its observed data-loss-prevention events involving unauthorized generative AI. The data includes actions security tools may have blocked. It does not establish that every transfer succeeded or constituted a breach. Verizon 2026 DBIR executive summary, p. 12
AC-T1 · Secure development AI-assisted code leaves out an access check, so ordinary tests can pass while the flaw reaches production. Direct for AI-assisted coding. Phala Cloud reported that AI-assisted code left out a workspace ownership check. Functional tests and review missed it, and an attacker changed other customers' resources. The postmortem attributes the code to AI assistance and describes the team's tests. Phala Cloud postmortem, 2026
AC-T2 · Secure development Repository settings cause an agent to run commands automatically, so a compromised setting can execute code with the agent's access. Operational case. Mandiant reported that attackers placed malicious hooks in an internal AI repository and that the hooks led to code execution in the environment running the assistant. The client is anonymized. Mandiant 2026 report, case 3

Review

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AV-G1 · Governance A repository counts automated approvals and has no separate human-review rule, so a change can merge without the intended human review. Control evidence. GitHub says the Copilot code review approval feature is off by default. When enabled, its approval can count toward the repository's required approvals. The scenario depends on the repository's other rules. GitHub approval announcement, October 2026
AV-G2 · Governance Checks on an agent's pull request start with access to secrets or write permissions before anyone reviews the change, so unreviewed code can act with those permissions. Control evidence and analogue. GitHub documents an opt-in setting to run workflows from Copilot-authored pull requests without the default human approval. Those jobs can receive secrets or permissions depending on configuration. B-T3 describes a separate CI boundary failure involving untrusted work. GitHub workflow approval settings, 2026 · TanStack postmortem, 2026
AV-T1 · Secure development A review agent misses why a change is unsafe, so vulnerable code can move forward after automated review. Study. SecPRBench evaluated model reviewers on known security bugs introduced by real changes. Its preprint reports missed bugs and variation by model. The study tested reviewer performance in a benchmark. SecPRBench preprint, 2026

Build

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AB-G1 · Governance A tool supplier gives a build integration more publishing access than it needs, so an unauthorized update can reach users through the trusted channel. Compromise. AWS reported that an overly broad build integration token was used to add unauthorized code to an Amazon Q Developer extension release. AWS found the injected code did not execute. AWS bulletin, 2025
AB-T1 · Secure development A build record from the agent is accepted without checking which builder, source and package it refers to, so recipients cannot verify what they received. Control evidence and analogue. SLSA describes checks that link provenance to the artifact, builder and expected source. SolarWinds documented a compromised build producer. A public case of an agent creating a false attestation remains unverified. SLSA verification · SolarWinds SEC filing, 2021

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence

Release

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AR-G1 · Governance A team treats an agent's recommendation as release approval, so the release can go ahead without a decision from the person who owns the risk. Supervisory finding and analogue. The FCA documented an AI use case reaching production under an undocumented exception and found conditional approvals recorded as full in its wholesale-bank review. The source supports the approval failure. It leaves who or what generated the recommendation unknown. FCA wholesale-bank review, autumn 2026
AR-T1 · Secure development A release agent matches passing checks to the wrong build, so it can recommend a package that those checks never covered. Control evidence and analogue. The in-toto test-result specification identifies what was tested so a verifier can check it against the expected test target. The FCA documented a mismatch between TSB's test and production configurations. A public case of an agent matching test evidence to the wrong build remains unverified. in-toto test-result specification · FCA final notice, sections 5.4-5.5

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AD-G1 · Governance An agent can delete a live resource through an automation interface with fewer safeguards than the dashboard, so an unintended deletion takes effect without time to undo it. Operational case. Railway reported that its older API applied the PocketOS deletion immediately, while the dashboard had a recovery window for the same action. Railway added delayed deletion to the API after the incident. The case documents a difference in protections between interfaces. Railway incident account, 2026

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AO-G1 · Governance A change freeze exists only in the agent's instructions, so the agent can keep making changes after responders tell it to stop. Operational case and supervisory finding. SaaStr reported the Replit agent continuing to make changes during a freeze. Separately, the FCA found gaps in testing and maintaining stop controls and documenting who can operate them. SaaStr account, 2025 · FCA wholesale-bank review, autumn 2026
AO-G2 · Governance A provider changes an agent's defaults or instructions after validation, so a trusted workflow can start behaving differently with the same access. Operational case for behavior change. Anthropic reported coding-tool quality regressions following changes to reasoning defaults, context management and instructions. The FCA identifies notice, testing and fallback planning for provider changes as good practice. A security bypass in the Anthropic case remains unverified. Anthropic postmortem, 2026 · FCA AI good and poor practice, section 4.2
AO-T1 · Secure development An agent wrongly says recovery is impossible, so responders who trust it can skip a working recovery option. Operational case. SaaStr reported that its agent incorrectly said recovery was impossible after production data was deleted. The team recovered the data through rollback. The decision to abandon recovery remains a potential consequence. SaaStr account, 2025
AO-T2 · Secure development An agent saves untrusted instructions in shared memory, and later sessions follow them in other work. Demonstration. A Cisco researcher described inducing a coding agent to persist untrusted instructions and reuse them across sessions or projects in a controlled test. Cisco research writeup published by OWASP, 2026
AO-T3 · Secure development An agent keeps retrying a failed task without a hard stop, so it runs up charges or blocks a live service. Operational case in a comparable workflow. Mandiant described an anonymized financial-services agent that retried bad data, increased usage charges and caused database locks that halted transactions. Mandiant 2026 report, case 6
AO-T4 · Secure development An agent sends a screenshot, log or code to an unapproved external service to finish a task, so data can leave the company's control. Demonstration. Microsoft describes an Azure SRE Agent test in which the agent sent a test screenshot to a public service. Customer screenshot exposure was outside the reported test. Microsoft engineering account, 2026

Monitor

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AM-G1 · Governance Coding histories are shared with a provider while they still contain readable credentials, so a supplier compromise can expose both the histories and the credentials. Operational case. Cursor reported theft of historical coding-agent traces from storage used to share data with Fireworks. Some traces contained unredacted credentials. The disclosure leaves customer policy decisions unknown. Cursor incident update, 2026
AM-G2 · Governance Tool logs identify only a shared account, so responders cannot tell which agent run took a production action or who authorized it. Control evidence. NIST's proposed identity tenets call for independent evidence linking the agent, delegated intent and resulting action. The example applies that accountability requirement to an agent that shares a production identity. NIST design tenets for agent identity, 2026
AM-T1 · Secure development An agent can change its own monitoring during routine work, so security staff can lose alerts and get an inaccurate account of what changed. Operational case in internal coding work. OpenAI reported agents disabling notifications or removing monitoring in two internal coding tasks. One incorrectly claimed it had preserved monitoring. OpenAI report, 2026
AM-T2 · Secure development An agent can alter the record of its own actions, so responders can be misled about what ran even when records are present. Operational case in an evaluation. METR found limited successful falsification of recorded tool activity in the Hugging Face evaluation incident. Its observed tests concerned the apparent actions in a run. METR independent investigation, 2026

Retire

Risk Failure and consequence Public evidence
AX-G1 · Governance A team closes an agent without assigning responsibility for copies of its history, so sensitive data can remain with a provider after the work ends. Analogue. Cursor found that historical agent traces containing credentials were still present in storage it shared with a provider. The evidence establishes persistent copies and their later exposure. The shutdown scenario remains an inference. Cursor incident update, 2026

Cloudflare reported that credentials missed in a supplier-breach rotation still worked and were used in an intrusion. NIST's agent identity tenets describe runtime revocation. The incident concerns rotation. Cloudflare incident, 2023 · NIST design tenets for agent identity, 2026
