# DevSecOps visual draft fact check

Checked: 10 November 2026

This draft uses a single vertical diagram to connect lifecycle phases, governance work and secure development activities. The diagram is described in `devsecops-visual.flow.txt`.

## Scope and verdict

The page is a high-level synthesis of the lifecycle descriptions supplied with the request. The governance risks are supported by public primary sources from NIST and OWASP. The phase descriptions and secure development activities reflect the supplied lifecycle outline. No external policy, regulatory interpretation or product capability is asserted.

## Interpretation by AI

The risk pills describe potential consequences of skipping the activity in the same lane. Governance consequences are framed as business impact; engineering consequences are framed as technical impact. This cause-and-consequence wording, as well as the phase mapping, is interpretation by AI. The cited passages establish the risk mechanism or the purpose of the control.

Verified 10 November 2026. All excerpts below match the linked primary sources. The data recovery example concerns media that should be sanitized at retirement.

### Governance risks

| Phase | Risk pill | Verified excerpt and source |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | No named risk owner, delaying decisions and leaving business exposure unresolved. | “Roles, responsibilities, and authorities related to cybersecurity risk management are established, communicated, understood, and enforced.” [NIST CSF 2.0 · GV.RR-02](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.29.pdf#page=17) |
| Plan | Critical service goes unclassified, leaving resilience planning and investment out of step with its business importance. | “Assets are prioritized based on classification, criticality, resources, and impact on the mission.” [NIST CSF 2.0 · ID.AM-05](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.29.pdf#page=18) |
| Plan | Sensitive data processing remains unknown, risking costly remediation and loss of customer trust if data is exposed. | “a description of the categories of data subjects and of the categories of personal data” [GDPR · Article 30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng#art_30) |
| Plan | DPIA need or completion goes unchecked, leaving privacy impacts unassessed and remediation costly. | “likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons” [GDPR · Article 35](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng#art_35) |
| Plan | Privacy compliance cannot be demonstrated, weakening audit assurance and putting contracts and customer trust at risk. | “to ensure and to be able to demonstrate that processing is performed in accordance with this Regulation.” [GDPR · Article 24](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng#art_24) |

| Code | Development starts without security requirements, leading to non-compliance and possibly exploitable vulnerabilities. | “Identify and document all security requirements for organization-developed software to meet, and maintain the requirements over time.” [NIST SSDF 1.1 · PO.1.2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-218.pdf#page=14) |
