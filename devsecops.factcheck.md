# DevSecOps visual draft fact check

Checked: 14 November 2026

This draft uses a single vertical diagram to connect lifecycle phases, governance work and secure development activities. The diagram is described in `devsecops.flow.txt`. The original source review was completed on 10 November 2026; the legal-obligations risk and its additional sources were reviewed on 11 November 2026. Every quote, link and page number was rechecked against its source on 14 November 2026.

## Scope and verdict

The ten phases and their assignments are an editorial lifecycle synthesis, not a claim that one universal lifecycle or mandatory order exists. OWASP SAMM defines five business functions (Governance, Design, Implementation, Verification, Operations), while NIST SSDF 1.1 defines four practice groups (Prepare the Organization, Protect the Software, Produce Well-Secured Software, Respond to Vulnerabilities). Neither defines this page's ten named phases. The mapping and the activity prompts below are interpretation by AI, not requirements stated by those frameworks. Their subjects are consistent with the cited framework practices: requirements and risk preparation; threat assessment; secure code and dependency practices; secure build and deployment; verification; operations, monitoring, patching and decommissioning. The release gate is a recommendation, not a universal policy. No external policy, regulatory interpretation or product capability is asserted.

## Interpretation by AI

The risk table lists possible consequences of skipping an activity in the same lane. Governance consequences are framed as business impact; engineering consequences are framed as technical impact. This cause-and-consequence wording and phase mapping are interpretation by AI. The quoted passages establish the risk mechanism or control purpose, not the probability, cost or certainty of each consequence. The table is illustrative, not exhaustive.

Existing risk excerpts were verified on 10 November 2026. The consolidated legal-obligations risk and its ISO/NIST sources were checked on 11 November 2026. GDPR references are conditional on the regulation applying and on the specific legal conditions. Article 30 has exceptions. A [proposal to amend its record-keeping threshold](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-10978-2026-INIT/en/pdf) (Omnibus IV, COM(2025) 501, procedure 2025/0130(COD)) is not enacted law. On 14 November 2026 the [European Parliament procedure file](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2025/0130%28COD%29) showed "Awaiting Parliament's position in 1st reading", with 23 November 2026 as the indicative plenary date. The data recovery example concerns data-bearing media that is due for sanitization, not data that must still be retained.

## Corrections in this review

- The lifecycle introduction now explicitly identifies the ten-stage map as a working interpretation, not a universal or prescribed sequence.
- Release guidance no longer implies that a system owner may accept any critical or high finding without process constraints. It calls for resolution or a time-limited exception approved by the authorized risk owner, with compensating controls where appropriate.
- The three separate GDPR risks were combined into one general legal and regulatory compliance risk. GDPR Articles 30, 35 and 24 remain as conditional examples inside its evidence details; they do not imply that all three provisions apply to every organization or processing activity.
- The Code supply-chain risk now describes a compromised or vulnerable dependency reaching downstream users. The cited NIST supply-chain definition and SSDF component-vulnerability practice support this wording; the previous claim about token theft and access to critical systems was too specific for its evidence.
- The retirement recovery risk now concerns data-bearing media due for sanitization and does not imply that retained data should be destroyed.
- Privacy compliance wording was narrowed to the controller's GDPR accountability obligation. The prior references to contracts and customer trust were not established by the cited Article 24 excerpt.
- Corrected NIST PDF destinations to the pages containing the quoted text and aligned the first-party code-testing excerpt with the exact SSDF PW.7 wording.
- Moved the missing-security-requirements risk from Governance to Secure Development under Code. NIST SSDF PO.1.2 supports the risk, while the lane assignment is an editorial classification. Superseded on 14 November 2026: the risk now sits under Plan, see below.
